So is there any AI in it at all?

A small one, under the bonnet, and we'll always tell you. It reads your draft and points at the line that works, then explains why. That's it. It's never allowed to write, rewrite or "fix" a single word of yours, and a filter strips out anything that looks like it tried. We use a robot to make you less robotic.

    How we teach

    The only red line here is the margin.

    Six rules for how we teach. They're there to get you thinking and creating, not memorising rules.

    Mistakes are the best part.

    Your brain learns most when it gets something a bit wrong and then notices. A mistake is your brain doing push-ups. Try something brave that doesn't quite land and you'll earn a Best Mistake badge, with a note on why it was still a smart try.

    1. Fun first

      If you're laughing, you're learning. Every lesson opens with a joke, a silly example or a funny challenge.

    2. You write. We never correct.

      No red pen, no crossed-out words, no marks out of 10 on your daily drafts. Your draft is yours. We never touch it.

    3. Teach the why

      We don't say "fix this". We show why one line works and another goes flat, and why writers make the choices they do. Knowing the why gets you thinking, and thinking gets you creative.

    4. Make them think, then let them play

      Every lesson ends with a question or a challenge with no right answer. Once you know the why, you can break the rule on purpose. That's where creativity starts.

    5. Show, with examples

      Every idea comes with a version that works, one that doesn't, and what's going on in the reader's head.

    6. Talk like a good teacher

      Short sentences, everyday words, no jargon. If a 12-year-old wouldn't get it, we rewrite it.
